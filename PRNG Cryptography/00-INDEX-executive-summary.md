## Executive Summary

ARX (Addition-Rotation-XOR) cryptography occupies a specific, durable niche: cryptographic permutations and ciphers built **exclusively** from modular addition, bit rotation, and XOR — no S-box lookup tables at all. 🟢 **High confidence.** This buys two properties SPN designs (AES) don't get for free: **constant-time execution on ordinary CPUs without special hardware** (no table lookups means no cache-timing side channel of the classic software-AES kind), and **excellent software performance on general-purpose processors**, especially where AES-NI-class hardware acceleration isn't available. The cost is that ARX's only source of nonlinearity — modular addition's carry chain — is harder to bound formally than a designed S-box's differential/linear tables, which is *why* ARX cryptanalysis and ARX-specific automated-search tooling (MILP/SAT modeling of addition specifically) became their own substantial research subfield rather than simply inheriting AES-era techniques wholesale.

The field's current, well-defined body of knowledge splits cleanly into three tiers a researcher needs, in order: **(1) the operation-level mathematics** — exactly how a difference (XOR-, additive-, or rotational-XOR-) propagates through modular addition, including the carry-dependent conditional probability that makes ARX differential cryptanalysis fundamentally bit-position-correlated in a way SPN differential cryptanalysis isn't; **(2) the automated-search toolchain** — MILP (via Gurobi/CPLEX) and SAT/SMT (via CryptoMiniSat, STP, and purpose-built frameworks like CryptoSMT and, as of 2023–2025, CLAASP and the "Window Heuristic") that have become the *only* practical way to find optimal or near-optimal differential/linear trails once word size exceeds toy scale, fully displacing hand search for any serious current publication; and **(3) design-side judgment** — how to choose rotation constants, round counts, and mixing schedules that give a *provable* security margin via the wide-trail (SPN) or long-trail (ARX-adapted, per the Sparkle/Alzette design) strategy, rather than merely "surviving" whatever attacks the designer personally thought to try.

**What's genuinely live and current, as of 2026, and load-bearing for anyone wanting to publish now, not five years ago:** (1) ChaCha's best published attack has crept from 7 rounds to **7.5 rounds** (of 20) across 2023–2025 via a steady sequence of differential-linear refinements — Beierle et al.'s CRYPTO 2020 3.5-round differential, Bellini et al.'s ToSC 2023 two-bit-difference distinguisher, Xu et al.'s ToSC 2024 7.25/7.5-round result, and Flórez-Gutiérrez & Todo's EUROCRYPT 2025 "bit puncturing" technique — none of which threatens deployed ChaCha20's full 20 rounds, but which is the exact, current state of the art any new ChaCha-family design proposal must be benchmarked against. (2) **NIST finalized Ascon as SP 800-232 on August 13, 2025** — an SPN-sponge hybrid (not pure ARX; its S-box layer is a fixed 5-bit nonlinear substitution, not addition-derived nonlinearity), but its ARX-*adjacent* competitor Sparkle/Alzette (an actual ARX-box, CRYPTO 2020) reached the NIST lightweight-competition final ten before losing out — a genuinely instructive real case study in why a design with a formally-proven security margin (the long-trail strategy) can still lose a standardization competition to a different structural approach, covered in depth in Part 2 of this report (`02-...`). (3) **Automated trail search has itself become a fast-moving research area**, not a solved, stable toolchain — CLAASP (a unified automated-analysis library, ePrint 2023) and the "Window Heuristic" (CT-RSA 2025) both represent 2023–2025 advances specifically in *how well* MILP/SAT tooling handles ARX's addition operator, meaning tooling recommendations in older ARX papers/tutorials are measurably out of date. (4) **Rotational-XOR (RX) cryptanalysis** has matured from a 2016-era theoretical proposal into a standard, tooled attack class with 2023–2025 automated-search frameworks reaching materially more rounds than earlier hand-derived RX trails (e.g., 17–24-round RX-differential characteristics against Speck variants where prior best results reached only 13). (5) **Differential-neural cryptanalysis** — training a neural network directly as a differential distinguisher, pioneered by Gohr at CRYPTO 2019 against Speck32/64 — has become a mature, actively-competitive sub-line of ARX cryptanalysis through 2023–2025 (DbitNet's architecture-agnostic distinguishers, related-key extensions, and, most recently, 2025 work explicitly combining deep learning with rotational-XOR cryptanalysis specifically against Simon/Simeck), and is the concrete, ARX-specific instance of the broader AI-in-cryptanalysis trend that (per this session's earlier, separate cryptography report) produced a verified, standards-altering result against a NIST PQC signature candidate in July 2026 — the two threads are different techniques applied to different targets, but any 2026 ARX researcher needs literacy in the differential-neural line specifically, not just awareness that "AI cryptanalysis is a thing."

**What separates a competent ARX implementer from a researcher capable of designing and publishing a novel permutation**, per this report's synthesis: an implementer needs to correctly deploy an existing, well-analyzed design (ChaCha20, BLAKE3) with correct nonce/counter discipline. A researcher needs to be fluent enough in modular addition's exact differential/linear probability structure to build or operate an MILP/SAT model of a *new* design's round function, needs the judgment to select round counts and rotation constants that survive that model's best trail search with genuine margin (not merely "more rounds than everyone else uses"), needs to know the diffusion-metric literature well enough to *not* mistake avalanche/SAC compliance for actual differential-security (a real, named, recurring mistake covered in Part 8 of this report), and needs to understand exactly what a CRYPTO/FSE/ToSC/CHES reviewer will demand as evidence before treating a new construction as a genuine contribution rather than an unanalyzed toy. That is essentially the syllabus this report lays out.

## Consensus vs. Debate

**Consensus:** modular addition's carry-chain nonlinearity is real but structurally weaker per-bit than a well-designed S-box, which is why ARX designs compensate with more rounds relative to their SPN counterparts for comparable margin; MILP/SAT automated search is now mandatory, not optional, evidence for any new design's security claims at a serious venue; the long-trail strategy is the correct current framework for giving an ARX permutation a provable (not merely empirical) differential/linear bound; differential-neural distinguishers are a genuine, competitive cryptanalytic technique, not a novelty.

**Active debate:** (1) *How much of differential-neural distinguishers' power comes from genuinely exploiting structure beyond the classical differential distribution table*, versus from data-generation/training artifacts — Gohr's own "aaaa-blinding" experiment and subsequent "more insight" follow-up work (2023) is precisely an attempt to pin this down, and the question is not fully settled. (2) *Whether AND-RX and RX-generalized frameworks (extending rotational-XOR cryptanalysis beyond pure ARX to Simon/Simeck's AND-RX structure) represent a genuinely distinct attack surface designers must budget for separately*, or are adequately covered by standard differential/linear bounds — the 2023–2025 RX-tooling literature is actively pushing on exactly this question. (3) *Whether a NIST-style open competition (as ran for AES/SHA-3/PQC/lightweight-crypto) is the right validation bar for a genuinely new ARX permutation proposed by an independent researcher, versus incremental ToSC/FSE publication* — this is more a field-norms question than a cryptanalytic one, and is addressed directly in `08`.

## What's Missing / Gaps

- Some 2025–2026 IACR ePrint preprints referenced or implied by citation chains in this report's search record had not yet completed peer review at the time of writing; treat any specific complexity figure attributed to a 2025-dated paper as the currently-published claim, not as settled community consensus the way a multi-year-old CRYPTO result is.
- Rotation-constant search specifically for word sizes larger than 64 bits (128-bit-lane designs) is thinner in the current literature than 32/64-bit search — flagged explicitly in `04` as a genuine open area rather than glossed over.
- This report's neural-cryptanalysis coverage is grounded entirely in the Speck/Simon/Simeck line (where nearly all the published work sits); generalizing differential-neural techniques to sponge-based ARX permutations (Xoodoo, the Ascon-adjacent space) is comparatively unexplored in the current literature and is flagged as an open problem in `09`, not reported as an existing result.
- As with this conversation's earlier general-cryptography report, non-English (particularly Chinese-language) ARX cryptanalysis literature is underrepresented — worth flagging again here since several of the strongest 2023–2025 automated-search and RX papers found in this session's search record do originate from Chinese research groups publishing in English-language venues, suggesting the underlying research community is more geographically distributed than a casual reading of "Western IACR venues" would suggest.
