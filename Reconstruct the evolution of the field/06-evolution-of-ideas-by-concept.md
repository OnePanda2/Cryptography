# 06 — Evolution of Ideas: Cross-Cutting Threads, 1993–2026

*Part of a multi-file chronological history — see `00-INDEX-executive-summary.md` for the map. This file re-traces the same 33 years, but by concept rather than by year, cross-referencing the year-by-year chapters (`01`–`05`) rather than repeating their detail.*

---

## ARX

**Origin** (1994, `01`): RC5's data-dependent rotation, an early, not-yet-doctrinal use of rotation as a cryptographic primitive. **Founding, doctrinal moment** (2005, `02`): Salsa20, explicitly motivated by software AES's cache-timing weakness, establishing "no table lookups, ever" as ARX's core, deliberate value proposition rather than an incidental design choice. **Refinement without crisis** (2008, `02`): ChaCha's column/diagonal restructuring, proving the paradigm could improve through voluntary, analysis-driven iteration, not just attack-driven correction. **Generalization beyond stream ciphers** (2012, `03`): BLAKE2's ChaCha-derived G function, showing ARX ideas transplant cleanly into hash-function design. **Institutional validation** (2013–2018, `03`–`04`): eSTREAM's 2009 portfolio selection, then TLS 1.3's 2018 standardization of ChaCha20-Poly1305, cementing ARX as first-class internet infrastructure, not a niche alternative. **Provable-margin maturity** (2020, `04`): Sparkle/Alzette's long-trail strategy, closing the nineteen-year gap between AES's wide-trail-strategy rigor (2001) and ARX achieving the analogous proof standard. **Continued cryptanalytic pressure without capitulation** (2020–2025, `04`–`05`): the differential-linear/PNB/MILP/bit-puncturing lineage against ChaCha, creeping from 7 to 7.5 of 20 rounds — real, sustained, expert progress that has never once threatened the deployed cipher, the clearest available illustration of what "actively studied but still trusted" looks like in practice. **Where it stands in 2026**: the dominant paradigm for software-first, side-channel-conscious, hardware-acceleration-independent symmetric design, still riding the same underlying condition (uneven hardware-acceleration availability) that motivated its creation twenty-one years earlier, and still gaining performance for free from general-purpose SIMD-width growth rather than any dedicated silicon.

## SPN (Substitution-Permutation Networks)

**Established as the field's dominant symmetric paradigm** by AES's 2000 selection (`01`), specifically because Rijndael's wide-trail strategy delivered *provable* differential/linear bounds Serpent's comparably strong but less formally-argued margin didn't as decisively offer. **Institutionally re-validated** via Keccak's 2012 SHA-3 win (`03`), though Keccak's sponge *usage* of an SPN-style permutation is a structurally distinct question from the permutation's own internal SPN design — a distinction this report's evolution-of-ideas approach is specifically positioned to make clearly, since the year-by-year chapters necessarily entangled the two. **Its lasting methodological export**: the wide-trail strategy itself, whose direct 2020 ARX adaptation (the long-trail strategy, `04`) is arguably SPN design's single most consequential intellectual legacy to the *rest* of the field — a proof technique that outlived any specific cipher and became a transferable methodology.

## Feistel networks

The dominant paradigm entering this history's window (DES, 1977, pre-dating `01`'s start), formally justified by the Luby-Rackoff result (1988, pre-dating this report's window but foundational background) proving three rounds of Feistel with independent pseudorandom functions yield a pseudorandom permutation. **Its relative decline across this history's span**: while Feistel-structured designs persisted (3DES through the 1990s, Camellia in 2000, Speck's two-word Feistel-like structure in 2013), the *dominant* new-design paradigm shifted decisively toward SPN (AES, 2000) and, for the ARX-specific niche, toward permutation-style designs (Salsa20/ChaCha, 2005/2008) rather than Feistel structures — Speck (2013, `03`) is, tellingly, this history's last major Feistel-lineage design to achieve wide adoption and study, and even it sits alongside its AND-RX, equally-Feistel-like sibling Simon rather than representing a renewed general Feistel resurgence.

## Permutation-based cryptography, and the sponge/duplex construction

**Motivated directly by structural unease with Merkle–Damgård** building through 2002–2003 (`02`), specifically its length-extension weakness. **Founded** via Keccak's SHA-3 competition entry and the accompanying indifferentiability proof (Bertoni, Daemen, Peeters, Van Assche, 2008, `02`), giving the sponge construction — absorb input, squeeze output, via repeated application of a fixed permutation to a large state — a rigorous security foundation from day one, not a retrofit. **Institutionally validated** by Keccak's 2012 SHA-3 selection (`03`). **Generalized into the duplex construction** (interleaved absorb/squeeze in a single pass), enabling AEAD schemes built directly from a permutation without a separate cipher-plus-MAC composition — the design pattern underlying Ascon's eventual 2022–2025 NIST Lightweight Cryptography win (`04`–`05`) and Xoodyak's flexible "Cyclist" mode. **Where it stands in 2026**: alongside ARX, the field's other dominant structural paradigm for new symmetric design, with the two paradigms cross-pollinating rather than competing purely head-to-head — Xoodoo is a sponge permutation built from AND-RX operations, and Sparkle's ARX-box Alzette is wrapped into sponge-adjacent AEAD/hash modes (Schwaemm/Esch) — the "sponge vs. Merkle–Damgård" and "ARX vs. SPN" axes turning out to be genuinely orthogonal design choices, not the same choice viewed twice, a distinction this history's chronological chapters had to build up to gradually but which this cross-cutting view can state directly.

## Authenticated encryption (AEAD)

**The problem was recognized gradually through the 1990s–2000s** via a long, painful sequence of generic-composition and padding-oracle vulnerabilities (Vaudenay's original padding-oracle work and its many TLS-specific CBC-mode descendants), crystallizing into a rigorous fix via **Bellare and Namprempre's 2000 proof that Encrypt-then-MAC, uniquely among the three naive composition orderings, is generically secure** for any secure component choice — a result whose practical consequences (TLS's historical preference for the *insecure* MAC-then-Encrypt ordering in its CBC ciphersuites) took over a decade to fully unwind, not resolving until TLS 1.3 removed CBC-mode ciphersuites outright in 2018 (`04`). **The field's response escalated from proof to institution**: CAESAR (opened 2014, `03`) gave AEAD its own dedicated, competition-scale scrutiny for the first time, producing AES-GCM-SIV (nonce-misuse-resistant, directly answering AES-GCM's catastrophic nonce-reuse failure mode) and OCB among its notable outputs, and directly informing the design thinking behind the later NIST Lightweight Cryptography competition's AEAD-focused evaluation criteria (Ascon-AEAD128, Schwaemm, Xoodyak's AEAD mode). **Where it stands in 2026**: AEAD-as-a-single-primitive is now the unambiguous default expectation for any new symmetric-encryption use case — "just encrypt, don't separately MAC" is no longer even a live design question the way it genuinely was through the 2000s.

## Diffusion metrics

**Established early and largely unchanged in their basic form**: SAC and BIC (Webster and Tavares, 1985, pre-dating this report's window) remain the field's standard empirical diffusion-quality tests through 2026, essentially unmodified in their core statistical definition. **What changed is not the metrics themselves but the field's understanding of their limits**: the recurring, explicitly-named lesson (most sharply articulated across the ARX design literature this history's companion document covers in depth) that SAC/BIC/avalanche compliance is *necessary but nowhere near sufficient* evidence of differential/linear-cryptanalytic security — a distinction the field has become progressively stricter about enforcing as automated MILP/SAT trail search (below) matured into a mandatory complement, not a nice-to-have, meaning a 2026-era diffusion-metric section in a serious paper functions as supporting color, not primary evidence, in a way it arguably still could pass as in earlier decades.

## Randomness testing

**The test-suite landscape** (NIST STS, Dieharder, TestU01/BigCrush, PractRand) largely matured as a toolkit independent of this history's major cryptographic turning points — these are general-purpose statistical tools, not cryptography-specific inventions, and their core methodology didn't need to change when, say, SHA-1 fell or PQC standardization opened. **What sharpened, dramatically, is the field's understanding of what passing these tests does and doesn't prove**: the Dual_EC_DRBG episode (2006 standardization, 2007 theoretical-backdoor demonstration, 2013 Snowden-driven confirmation, `02`–`03`) stands as this entire history's starkest illustration that a backdoored generator's output is, by the backdoor's own design requirement, statistically indistinguishable from random to anyone without the secret trapdoor — meaning it would sail through every test suite in this section with no anomaly whatsoever. This single episode did more to permanently correct the field's (and the broader engineering community's) intuitions about "passing randomness tests equals cryptographic security" than any purely pedagogical argument could have, and its lesson is cited, explicitly, throughout this report's companion documents as the canonical cautionary tale.

## Stream ciphers

**RC4's long, slow fall** (widely deployed from the 1990s, statistical biases published progressively through the 2000s, formally prohibited in TLS via RFC 7465 in 2015) runs as a background thread through nearly this entire history, a cautionary tale about a design that looked adequate for over a decade before cryptanalysis caught up with real exploitability. **eSTREAM** (2004–2009, `02`–`03`) directly responded to the general unease this pattern created, producing Salsa20's software-portfolio validation as its most consequential legacy. **The paradigm's center of gravity shifted decisively toward ARX** across this history, to the point that "stream cipher" and "ARX design" are near-synonymous in current (2026) practice for any new general-purpose proposal, a genuinely different landscape than 1993's DES-dominated, DES-mode-derived stream-cipher thinking.

## Hash functions

**The single most dramatic paradigm shift traced in this entire history**: from Merkle–Damgård/Davies–Meyer as the unquestioned default (1993–2004) through the MD5/SHA-1 collapse (2004–2005, theoretically; 2017, practically, `02`–`04`) to the sponge construction's rise as a structurally distinct, length-extension-immune alternative (2007–2015, `02`–`03`) to, by 2020–2026, a landscape where ARX-derived designs (BLAKE2/3) and sponge-based designs (SHA-3, Ascon-Hash) coexist as parallel, both-still-actively-used options rather than one having fully displaced the other — SHA-2, despite sharing SHA-1's structural family and despite the field's explicit 2007 hedge against exactly this risk, never actually failed, and remains, as of 2026, the single most widely deployed hash function in the world, a genuinely interesting historical footnote: the hedge (SHA-3) was judged worth building, and was successfully built, without the underlying risk it hedged against ever materializing.

## Counter-based PRNGs

A comparatively quiet thread relative to this history's headline events: **Threefry and Philox** (Salmon, Moraes, Dror, Shaw, 2011, `03`) introduced counter-based, embarrassingly-parallel generation specifically for scientific/GPU computing, a genuinely different design goal (parallel statistical quality at scale) from mainstream CSPRNG design's adversarial-security goal — and this history's companion ARX-specific document flags, as of 2026, that this thread's *adversarial* cryptanalytic scrutiny remains comparatively thin relative to its statistical-testing track record, a real, still-open gap this cross-cutting view surfaces precisely because it never became a "major event" year in any single chapter above.

## Automatic cryptanalysis: MILP, SAT, and beyond

**This history's clearest example of a slow-building, ultimately field-transforming methodological thread**: from zero automated tooling (1993) through Mouha, Wang, Gu, Preneel's 2011 first MILP application (`03`) through Sun et al.'s 2014 full ARX-addition-operator extension (`04`) through a decade of steady refinement (independent-rounds-assumption critiques, the Window Heuristic's 2025 partial-linearization advance, `05`) to, by 2026, a field where MILP/SAT-derived trail-search evidence is considered *mandatory*, not optional, for any serious new-cipher security claim — a genuinely completed paradigm shift, unlike some of this history's still-unresolved threads. **Its natural successor thread, differential-neural cryptanalysis** (Gohr, 2019, `04`), is comparatively younger and, as of 2025–2026, still actively contested on exactly how much of its power is genuinely novel versus a repackaging of classical structure — an live, unresolved question this report inherits rather than closes, deliberately.

## Formal proofs: ROM, ideal permutation model, and security reductions

**The ROM's arc** (introduced 1993 as an enabling tool, `01`; its theoretical insufficiency rigorously proven 2004, `02`; never actually abandoned, but permanently reclassified field-wide from "proof" to "very strong heuristic evidence") is this history's cleanest illustration of the field's mature relationship with its own idealized proof techniques — using them pragmatically while being explicit and honest about their limits, rather than either naively trusting them or discarding them once their limits were shown. **The ideal permutation model and indifferentiability** (Bertoni, Daemen, Peeters, Van Assche, 2008, `02`, building on Maurer-Renner-Holenstein's general 2004 indifferentiability framework) extended this same pragmatic-idealization approach to the sponge construction specifically, and remains the standard justification framework for any new sponge-based design's security argument through 2026. **Security reductions generally** grew steadily more concrete and quantitative across this history — from largely asymptotic ("negligible advantage") statements in the 1990s toward the explicit, numeric, trail-search-tied "concrete security" bounds this report's companion ARX document identifies as current (2025–2026) mandatory reviewer expectation — a multi-decade tightening of what counts as an adequate security argument, not a single discrete event.

## Portable optimization

**Emerges as an explicit, first-class design consideration specifically alongside ARX's rise**: ChaCha's 2008 column/diagonal restructuring (`02`) was partly a SIMD-parallelism-aware decision, not purely a diffusion-rate one, and this dual motivation only grew more explicit over the following years — BLAKE3's 2020 Merkle-tree redesign (this history's companion ARX document, `02` §2.3) is the starkest later instance, restructuring an entire hash function's internal architecture specifically to expose more exploitable parallelism as SIMD register widths and core counts grew. **The hardware landscape this thread rode on** shifted meaningfully across the period: AES-NI's 2010 arrival (`03`) partially resolved the *original* problem ARX solved for x86 server/desktop contexts, while general-purpose SIMD width kept growing (SSE → AVX2 → AVX-512, and ARM's NEON → SVE) on a timeline that kept ARX's software performance competitive without any ARX-specific hardware ever needing to exist — a genuinely durable, structural asymmetry between AES's hardware-fast-path advantage and ARX's software-portability advantage that has persisted, essentially unchanged in its basic shape, from 2010 through 2026.
